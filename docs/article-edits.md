## (b) Approved factual corrections

1. **History (A Brief Origin of Morse Code):** “by 1906, SOS was the signal every ship could use to call for help” → SOS was adopted as the international distress signal in 1906.
2. **FAQ “What does SOS stand for?”:** SOS does not officially stand for any words. “Save Our Souls” is a popular explanation added later. In Morse code it is three short signals, three long signals, and three short signals: `... --- ...` (fixed four-dot typo on the final group).
3. **FAQ “How can I translate Morse code?”:** Output is text in the output field (not “converted into Morse code”).
4. **FAQ “How can I type normal letters in the Morse code?”:** Type letters in the Text box; they convert to dots and dashes automatically.
5. **FAQ “How can I learn Morse code?”:** Learn by sound; Play button; chart only to look a character up (with internal link on “chart”).
6. **American Morse table** (in `AmericanMorseBlock.astro`, not duplicated in HomeArticle): “Gaps inside a letter” → “Extra pauses inside a letter”; International cell → “None. The gap between signals inside a letter is always one unit”.
